SSS Modernization Demo

A working platform for the Selective Service System's registrant services: online accounts, exemption eligibility, case management with appeals, and automatic compliance checks on every decision.

Demo site sss-demo-frontend.onrender.com Status as of October 14, 2026 Working in the demo 3 of 5 R0 stories, 11 of 11 R1 stories

Everything described here is demonstrated on a synthetic demo deployment; none of it is operating in production. Synthetic data only. The exemption types shown (A senior, B income, C hardship) are illustrative rules built to exercise the workflow. They are not Selective Service policy.

What the app is for

One place to register, apply, decide and prove it

The Selective Service System keeps the national registry that federal law requires nearly every man living in the United States to join between the ages of 18 and 25. Registration status follows people for years: it can affect eligibility for federal jobs and some state programs, so registrants, agencies and staff all need fast, accurate answers.

This demo modernizes the business systems behind that work: online registration and registrant records, profile updates with compliance checks, exemption and appeal case management, compliance verification, and reporting. It shows the user experience and the controls together, so evaluators can see how the proposed approach works in practice rather than on paper.

Registrants and applicants

Create an account, keep their profile current, see which exemptions they qualify for and why, apply, follow every step of their case, and appeal a denial.

Case managers

Start from a work list of their own cases, take ownership, decide with a stated reason, keep internal notes, and export case lists for reporting.

Compliance, administrators and leadership

See whether decisions follow the rules, review any that do not, and search two audit logs. Administrators see the whole operation and set access rules; leadership sees program numbers with no applicant data.

Challenges it solves

The problems this platform addresses

Registrants can't see where they stand

People need to register, update their details and know the status of a request without calling or mailing anyone.

Self-service accounts, a profile with a built-in compliance check, eligibility results with a plain-language reason for each, a case timeline and in-app notifications at every status change.

In the demo

Decisions that are hard to explain or challenge

Applicants deserve to know why they were refused and how to respond.

Rules produce a status and a reason for every exemption type. A denial cannot be saved without a written reason, which the applicant sees, and a denied applicant can appeal from the same page.

In the demo

No proof that each decision followed the rules

Oversight needs evidence for every decision, not a sample.

Every approval and denial is checked against 8 decision controls the moment it is made. A failure opens a review that only a different case manager can resolve, with a written justification. A dashboard tracks the rate against a 99% target.

In the demo

Sensitive personal data at risk

Social Security numbers and case records must be protected and only seen by the right people.

SSNs are stored only as one-way hashes, never shown back or logged, and entered in a masked field. Every page of the API declares who may use it, an automated test fails the build if any route lacks its access check, and every read, export and refusal goes into a write-once security audit log.

In the demo

Case work spread across inboxes and spreadsheets

Staff need one queue with clear ownership.

A shared case queue with status counts, filters, assignment, internal notes, document links and CSV export that never includes SSNs.

In the demo

Screens that show stale data

Dashboards and case pages must show current information within seconds of a change.

Every profile save, eligibility check, application and case change is timed until the next request can read it. Staff see each data type against a 30-second target for 95% of updates, an alert when one falls behind, and the slowest updates.

In the demo

Screens some people cannot use

Every screen must work for people using assistive technology (Section 508).

Every page is checked against WCAG 2.1 AA, which includes Section 508, signed out, as an applicant and as a case manager. It works by keyboard with a skip link, screen readers hear each field by its name with its errors, contrast is at least 4.5:1, nothing is lost at 320 pixels, a session warns before it ends, and applications are confirmed before they are sent.

In the demo

A stolen password is enough to sign in

Staff and registrants need a second proof of identity.

Two-step verification with an authenticator app: after the password, a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds, never works twice and gets five tries per sign-in. Every step is audited without the key or the code.

In the demo

Managers and leaders can't see the work

Each role needs the numbers that match its job, and no more.

Case managers get a work list with what is waiting on them and what is overdue. Administrators see every case by status and type, team performance and system health. Leadership sees approval rate, processing time and bottlenecks, with no applicant data.

In the demo

Access rules buried in code

Who may see or do what must be explicit, testable and changeable without a release.

Open Policy Agent decides every API request before any route runs, from role, resource, action and owner. Administrators change the rules on a page, with a reason, and every change and refusal is audited.

In the demo

Hosting that meets federal requirements

Production must run on FedRAMP-authorized cloud with multi-factor sign-in.

The demo runs on commercial hosting today. AWS GovCloud infrastructure (STORY-004) waits on approval. Multi-factor sign-in is already in the demo (scene 13).

Demo walkthrough

Seventeen scenes, about twenty minutes

  1. A registrant creates an account

    • The banner reminds everyone this is synthetic data. The SSN field is masked, with a Show toggle.
    • Passwords need 12 characters with a capital, a digit and a symbol. The SSN is hashed before it is stored.

  2. Eligibility, with a reason for every result

    • The profile page checks the registrant's record (18 or older, phone, address) and shows what is missing.
    • Each exemption type returns Eligible, Pending Review or Not Eligible with a plain-language reason. The registrant applies in one click and sees the application's status below.

  3. Case managers work one queue

    • Counts by status, filters by status, type, case manager and applicant, and an export of exactly the filtered cases.
    • Each case shows who owns it. Unassigned work is visible at a glance.

  4. A denial always carries its reason

    • The case manager cannot deny without a reason, and the applicant sees it on the timeline.
    • The applicant can appeal from the same page, which sends the case back for review.

  5. Every decision is checked automatically

    • When a case is approved or denied, 8 controls run at once: eligibility basis, a fresh eligibility check, reason and notice, separation of duties, ownership, review before decision, required document and an audit entry.
    • Here one of three decisions failed two controls, so the rate is 66.67% against the 99% target and the dashboard raises an alert.

  6. A second case manager reviews the failure

    • The case was approved while unassigned and without proof of age. The case page lists each control with its evidence.
    • The manager who decided cannot clear their own review. A different manager must write a justification, then either accept the exception (logged as an override) or reopen the case, which notifies the applicant.

  7. Auditors search every control result

    • Filter by date range, applicant or decider, decision, control and result.
    • Failed decisions stay in the record even after review, so the history cannot be tidied up afterwards.

  8. The applicant sees everything in one place

    • The dashboard lists each application with its status and the next step, the eligibility result for each exemption with its reason, the latest events across all cases, and notifications.
    • Applicants never see internal notes, assignments or compliance details; the dashboard and the case page share one rule for what an applicant may see.

  9. Every change is timed until it is readable

    • Each profile save, eligibility check, application and case change is timed from the start of the write until a separate read sees it, the way the next request would.
    • The target is 30 seconds for 95% of updates. Here one profile save waited 31 seconds on a database row held locked by another transaction, so profile updates went over target and the page raised the alert. The slowest-updates list shows the time went into the write.
    • Check now times a write end to end from the browser and separates server time from network time.

  10. Every security event is on the record

    • Sign-ins, including failed ones filed under the address tried, sign-outs, refusals, reads, exports, profile changes, decisions, role grants, deployments and configuration changes, each with who, when and from where.
    • The database refuses to edit or delete an entry, even for its owner, and keeps each one seven years. SSNs, passwords, codes and secrets never enter the log; a profile change lists the fields, not the values.

  11. Everyone can use it

    • Every page works from the keyboard. The first Tab offers Skip to main content, and focus is always visible.
    • Screen readers hear each field by its name; after a failed submit each field is marked invalid with its message, and focus moves to the first. Two minutes before a session ends, a warning offers Stay signed in.
    • An automated audit checks every page against WCAG 2.1 AA in Chrome. Run on the code before this work, it failed every page; now all twelve pass.

  12. Staff access is granted, never claimed

    • Access is given in the app, with a required reason, by an existing case manager (case-manager access only) or an administrator (any staff role). Registering an address no longer makes anyone staff.
    • Nobody can remove their own access, the last case manager's or the last administrator's. Every change is recorded in the Security Audit Log, and the person is notified.

  13. Sign-in asks for a second step

    • Under Sign-in security, a person scans a QR code with an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy or Microsoft Authenticator) or types the key. The key is shown once.
    • After the password, the person gets a pending sign-in that every page and route refuses, and has five minutes to enter the 6-digit code. A code works once, five wrong tries end the sign-in, and turning the second step off needs a code.
    • Every step is in the audit log without the key or the code. Our own review found a pending sign-in could be redeemed twice; it is now single use, and an end-to-end check that reproduced the gap failed on the old code.

  14. A case manager's day starts with their work list

    • My Work shows the manager's open cases, what is waiting on them (cases to start, cases to decide, compliance reviews of other managers' decisions), the unassigned queue and anything overdue, meaning no update for more than 5 days.
    • Recent decisions are listed below. A search opens the case list already filtered, with the filters in the address so the view can be shared.

  15. Administrators see the whole operation

    • All cases by status and type in charts and tables, and team performance: open cases, decided cases, median days to decide and compliance pass rate for each case manager.
    • System health sits on the same page: database and migrations, the deployed commit, freshness against 30 seconds, compliance against 99%, refusals in the last 24 hours and staff by role. Administrators also give and remove staff access, never their own and never the last administrator.

  16. Leadership sees the program, not the people

    • Total applications, approval rate, average processing time, open cases and appeal rate, with 12-week trends.
    • Bottlenecks by stage show the average days in each and how many cases are stuck for more than 5 days. The page holds aggregates only, no applicant data, and leadership does no case work.

  17. Access rules that change without a release

    • Open Policy Agent decides every API request before any route runs, from the person's role, the kind of record, the action and who owns or is assigned the case. One rule is the story's own example: case managers can view and work cases assigned to them.
    • Here a case manager opens a case assigned to someone else and is refused, with the rule named. The refusal is in the audit log with the rule and the policy version. The policy can refuse what the code allows, never allow what the code refuses.
    • Administrators change the rules on an Access Policies page, without a deploy: switch a rule off or on, narrow the routes it covers, give a reason. Each version is kept and cannot be edited, each change is audited, and it applies at once. Sign-in, health and the policy pages are locked so administrators cannot lock themselves out.
    • The earlier "API governance" service claimed to enforce OPA but never called it. We found that in our own archive and removed it.

Security and trust

Built to be checked

  • Protected identity data. Passwords and SSNs are stored as bcrypt hashes. The SSN is never returned by the API, never written to logs and left out of every export.
  • Access decided per endpoint. Each route declares whether it is public, for signed-in users, for the record's owner or for staff. A test fails the build when a route is missing its rule or its check.
  • Separation of duties. Case managers cannot decide their own cases or clear reviews of their own decisions.
  • Staff access granted, not claimed. Access is given in the app, with a reason, by an existing case manager or an administrator, never by registering an address; nobody can change their own access, and every change is in the audit log.
  • Two steps to sign in. An authenticator app supplies a 6-digit code after the password. The pending sign-in is refused everywhere, each code works once, and the key and codes never enter the log.
  • A policy decides every request. Open Policy Agent, compiled to WebAssembly inside the API, decides each request before any route and can only narrow what the code allows. Administrators change the rules without a deploy, and every version, change and refusal is on the record.
  • Accessible by design. Every page is checked against WCAG 2.1 AA (Section 508) for each kind of user: keyboard, screen-reader names and errors, contrast, reflow at 320 pixels, a session warning, and confirmation before an application is sent.
  • Write-once audit trail. Every sign-in, refusal, data read, export, change, role grant, deployment and configuration change is recorded with who, when and from where, alongside every compliance result. The database refuses edits and deletions, entries are kept seven years, and no SSN, password or secret is written to the log.
  • Verified releases. After each deploy the team checks the demo deployment automatically: the running version, the database, all protected routes refusing anonymous requests, and every staff route refusing an ordinary registrant. Each story ships with screenshots and a written conclusion.

Status and roadmap

11 of 11 in the demo (complete)
